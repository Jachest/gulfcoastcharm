# Gulfcoast Charm — Recreation Waiver and Court Rental Agreement

Draft for attorney review. Two documents follow:

- **Part A, Recreation Waiver and Release of Liability.** Signed by every adult who uses the court, whether an overnight guest, a day renter, or an invited player. One copy per adult. Parents or guardians sign for minors.
- **Part B, Court Rental Terms.** Signed by the person booking a day-use court rental. Incorporates Part A.

For overnight stays, paste Part A into the OwnerRez rental agreement as its own section titled "Recreation Waiver" so the booking guest signs it with the agreement, and have the booking guest collect Part A from every other adult player through the OwnerRez guest portal or a separate e-sign link.

Before using these, read the notes at the end on insurance, the statutory minor notice, and what to have a Florida attorney confirm.

## NOTES FOR THE OWNER (do not include in the signed document)

### Insurance, before the first day rental

This is the most important item on this page. A waiver reduces risk; insurance pays claims.

- **Day rentals to non-guests are a commercial activity** that most homeowner and many short-term-rental policies exclude. Call your insurer and ask, in these words, whether liability coverage applies to paid, hourly use of a sports court by people who are not overnight guests. Get the answer in writing.
- If it does not, you need a commercial general liability policy or an endorsement covering recreational facility rental, typically with at least $1,000,000 per occurrence, and a personal umbrella on top. Specialty short-term-rental insurers (Proper, Steadily, CBIZ and similar) write this kind of coverage.
- For overnight guests, confirm your STR policy specifically lists the volleyball court and the dock as covered amenities and does not exclude "sports courts" or "waterfront."

### The minor notice

The capitalized Notice to the Minor Child's Natural Guardian is the language Florida requires under section 744.301(3) of the Florida Statutes for a parental waiver of inherent risks in a commercial activity. For it to work, the statute requires the notice to appear in uppercase type at least 5 points larger than the surrounding text, and the released party must be named in the blanks exactly. Keep it verbatim, keep the formatting, and have the attorney confirm the current statutory wording, since it is the one clause where a word out of place can void the protection. Note that even a compliant parental waiver covers only the inherent risks of the activity, not the owner's negligence, which Florida does not allow a parent to waive for a child.

### What to have a Florida attorney confirm, about an hour of their time

1. The release language in Sections 2 and 3 meets Florida's clear-and-unequivocal standard for releasing the owner's own negligence, including the use of the word "negligence" in the operative sentences.
2. The minor notice and its formatting are current.
3. Whether to hold the Property in an LLC and name the LLC as the Released Party, which separates the house from your other assets.
4. The attorneys' fees, venue and limitation-of-liability clauses as applied to consumers.
5. Whether local ordinances in South Pasadena or Pinellas County restrict hourly commercial rental of a residential amenity, and whether the day-rental activity affects your short-term-rental license, zoning status or tourist development tax obligations.

### Operations

- Signatures are only as good as their collection. Require every adult to sign before stepping on the sand, not after. OwnerRez e-sign for overnight bookings; a free e-sign tool such as the OwnerRez guest form, Jotform or SignNow for additional players and day rentals, with a QR code posted at the court.
- Keep signed waivers for at least four years after the visit, which matches Florida's general negligence limitation period, and longer for minors, who can sue after reaching 18.
- Post an abbreviated Court Rules sign at the court. Signs help; they do not replace signatures.
- Fill every bracketed blank before use. Blanks in a signed contract are read against the drafter.
